A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Kansas City, Kansas City Restoration Co answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Sewage Cleanup FAQ

Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Stop using sinks, toilets and appliances that drain, since more water makes the backup worse. Turn off power and the HVAC system to the affected area only if you can do so without touching contaminated water. Do not try to clean it yourself, and wash thoroughly if you touched anything.
What each item is made of decides that. Glass, sealed tile, metal and other hard surfaces that do not absorb water are normally salvageable after disinfection. Porous materials that contacted sewage, including carpet, padding, drywall, insulation, mattresses and upholstered furniture, have to be discarded. Each discarded item is documented, giving you a record to use with your insurer.
In Kansas City, a sewage cleanup usually runs $2,000 to $10,000 or more, and the total reflects how large the affected area is and how far contamination spread. A small backup that stayed contained might be $1,500 to $3,000, but widespread sewage flooding can pass $15,000 once replacement materials and reconstruction are counted.
We typically recommend temporary relocation during sewage cleanup, especially if the contamination in your home is extensive. Airborne pathogens and the chemicals used for decontamination can both pose health risks. After seeing your situation firsthand, our team tells you what makes sense.
Removing the sewage and decontaminating the property is usually done in 1 to 3 days. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Our first priority is making the property safe to occupy again as fast as we can.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
