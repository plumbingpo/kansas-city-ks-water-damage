How Drying Works on a Lawrence Water Damage Job

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. Our job on every Lawrence property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

Extraction is always step one, since pumping and vacuuming remove water much faster than any drying machine. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. For most Lawrence homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. The equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not when the calendar does.

Where Water Damage Starts in Lawrence Properties

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Lawrence households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Lawrence, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.

How Lawrence Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A large share of the jobs we attend in Lawrence began as a minor fault that could have been caught. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Begin at the hoses. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and Lawrence homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.

Found Water in Your Lawrence Home? Start Here

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Lawrence jobs, with the better option for each.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. It is an electric shock risk and it will ruin the machine; use towels and a mop. Leave ceiling fixtures off if the ceiling above is wet, and keep away from any part that droops. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Do not turn the heat up high to dry things out, because warm, damp air is what mold likes.

The helpful things are simple: close the valve, prop open cupboards and wardrobes, and stand wet cushions and textiles up to drain. Do take pictures before moving anything.
